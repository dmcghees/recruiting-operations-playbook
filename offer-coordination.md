# Offer Coordination

## Purpose

Offer coordination connects the final hiring decision with the candidate's transition into the organization.

This guide outlines a practical process for supporting approvals, offer documentation, candidate communication, acceptance tracking, and handoff to onboarding.

## Offer Coordination Principles

Offer information should be:

* Accurate
* Approved
* Confidential
* Consistent
* Clearly documented

Only authorized employees should make compensation commitments or modify offer terms.

## 1. Confirm Hiring Decision

Before beginning the offer process, confirm:

* Final candidate selected
* Hiring manager approval
* Required interview feedback completed
* Position remains approved
* Compensation information is available

## 2. Verify Offer Details

Confirm information such as:

* Candidate name
* Job title
* Department
* Manager
* Work location
* Work arrangement
* Compensation
* Start date
* Employment classification
* Bonus or incentive information when applicable

## 3. Obtain Required Approvals

Depending on the organization, approvals may involve:

* Hiring manager
* Recruiting
* Human Resources
* Finance
* Compensation team
* Department leadership

Track outstanding approvals so the candidate is not delayed unnecessarily.

## Offer Approval Tracker

| Item | Owner | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hiring decision | Hiring Manager | Complete |
| Compensation | HR / Compensation | Complete |
| Budget approval | Finance | Complete |
| Start date | Hiring Manager | Complete |
| Offer document | HR / Recruiting | In Progress |

## 4. Prepare Offer Documentation

Use approved company systems and templates.

Verify the document before sending.

Check:

* Candidate name spelling
* Job title
* Compensation
* Start date
* Work location
* Manager
* Any approved additional terms

Sensitive offer information should not be stored in unapproved systems.

## 5. Candidate Communication

The candidate should understand:

* Offer process
* Expected timeline
* Who will contact them
* How the offer will be delivered
* Deadline for response when applicable
* Who to contact with questions

## 6. Track Offer Status

Possible statuses include:

* Preparing
* Awaiting approval
* Approved
* Extended
* Candidate reviewing
* Accepted
* Declined

Example:

| Candidate | Role | Offer Status | Date Extended | Response Due | Next Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Candidate A | Operations Coordinator | Candidate Reviewing | Oct 8 | Oct 12 | Follow up Oct 11 |

## 7. Offer Changes

If the candidate requests a change:

1. Document the request.
2. Route it to the authorized decision maker.
3. Avoid making commitments before approval.
4. Update documentation if the change is approved.
5. Confirm the final terms with the candidate.

## 8. Offer Acceptance

After acceptance:

* Confirm signed documentation
* Update candidate status
* Confirm final start date
* Notify required internal teams
* Begin onboarding handoff
* Close outstanding recruiting tasks

## 9. Candidate Decline

If the candidate declines:

* Document the outcome
* Record available reason information appropriately
* Notify the hiring manager
* Determine whether another candidate remains active
* Update recruiting records

## 10. Recruiting to Onboarding Handoff

Once the offer is accepted, provide the required information to the onboarding process.

This may include:

* Employee name
* Role
* Department
* Manager
* Start date
* Work location
* Work arrangement

Sensitive recruiting information should only be transferred when required and appropriate.

## Example

**Situation:** A candidate was selected, but the offer could not be issued because final compensation approval was still pending.

**Action:** Tracked the approval, followed up with the appropriate owner, kept the recruiting team informed, and avoided providing the candidate with unapproved compensation information.

**Result:** Once approval was completed, the offer could be prepared using confirmed terms.

## Key Takeaway

Offer coordination requires accuracy, confidentiality, clear ownership, and timely follow up.

The goal is to move from hiring decision to accepted offer without introducing preventable administrative delays or inaccurate information.
