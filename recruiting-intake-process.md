# Recruiting Intake Process

## Purpose

A strong recruiting process starts with a clear intake.

This guide outlines a practical process for gathering the information needed to open and support a hiring request before candidate outreach or interview coordination begins.

The goal is to reduce confusion, clarify ownership, improve candidate communication, and prevent avoidable delays later in the hiring process.

## Intake Objectives

A recruiting intake should confirm:

* Why the role is being opened
* What the team needs
* Who owns hiring decisions
* What qualifications matter most
* What the interview process will look like
* What compensation information is available
* What timeline the team is working toward
* What approvals or dependencies are still outstanding

## 1. Confirm the Hiring Request

Start by confirming basic role information.

This may include:

* Job title
* Department
* Hiring manager
* Employment type
* Work location
* Remote, hybrid, or onsite expectations
* Target start date
* Headcount approval
* Compensation range
* Reporting structure

Incomplete role information should be identified before the hiring process moves forward.

## 2. Understand the Business Need

Ask why the role is being opened.

Examples may include:

* New headcount
* Backfill
* Team growth
* Increased workload
* New business function
* Temporary coverage
* Replacement for an employee departure

Understanding the reason for the hire helps clarify urgency and priorities.

## 3. Define the Role

Work with the hiring manager to identify:

* Core responsibilities
* Required skills
* Preferred skills
* Experience level
* Education requirements when applicable
* Technical knowledge
* Schedule expectations
* Travel requirements
* Physical requirements when relevant

Separate true requirements from preferences.

## 4. Identify Priority Qualifications

Not every qualification should carry equal weight.

Ask the hiring manager to identify:

### Must Have

Qualifications a candidate needs in order to be considered.

### Strong Preference

Qualifications that improve fit but are not required.

### Nice to Have

Skills or experience that may be useful but should not unnecessarily narrow the candidate pool.

This helps reduce inconsistent candidate evaluation later.

## 5. Confirm Compensation

Document available compensation information.

This may include:

* Base salary or hourly range
* Bonus eligibility
* Commission when applicable
* Equity
* Benefits
* Location based compensation rules

Compensation should be communicated consistently according to company policy and applicable law.

## 6. Establish the Interview Process

Define the expected interview stages before candidates begin interviewing.

Example:

| Stage | Interviewer | Purpose | Length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recruiter Screen | Recruiting | Background and interest | 30 minutes |
| Hiring Manager Interview | Hiring Manager | Experience and role fit | 45 minutes |
| Team Interview | Team Members | Collaboration and functional skills | 45 minutes |
| Final Interview | Department Leader | Final evaluation | 30 minutes |

## 7. Define Evaluation Areas

Each interview should have a purpose.

Potential evaluation areas may include:

* Relevant experience
* Technical knowledge
* Communication
* Problem solving
* Project management
* Customer orientation
* Leadership
* Collaboration

Avoid having every interviewer evaluate the same things without a clear reason.

## 8. Confirm Interviewers

For each interviewer:

* Confirm participation
* Confirm calendar availability
* Clarify interview purpose
* Confirm backup interviewer when possible
* Provide interview guidance
* Explain feedback expectations

Interview delays often happen when interviewer ownership is unclear.

## 9. Establish Feedback Expectations

Agree on expectations for interview feedback.

Example:

* Feedback submitted within 24 hours
* Written comments required
* Interviewers evaluate assigned areas
* Final decision owner identified

Timely feedback reduces candidate delays and keeps the process moving.

## 10. Candidate Communication

Confirm what candidates should know throughout the process.

This may include:

* Interview stages
* Interview format
* Expected timeline
* Location or video meeting details
* Preparation information
* Point of contact
* Scheduling changes

Candidates should not have to guess what happens next.

## 11. Identify Process Risks

Potential risks may include:

* Interviewers with limited availability
* Aggressive hiring timeline
* Compensation not approved
* Job description still changing
* Unclear decision maker
* Too many interview stages
* Limited candidate pool
* Delayed feedback history

Documenting risks early makes them easier to manage.

## Sample Recruiting Intake Checklist

- [ ] Job title confirmed
- [ ] Department confirmed
- [ ] Hiring manager confirmed
- [ ] Headcount approved
- [ ] Compensation confirmed
- [ ] Work arrangement confirmed
- [ ] Target start date confirmed
- [ ] Required qualifications identified
- [ ] Preferred qualifications identified
- [ ] Interview stages defined
- [ ] Interviewers confirmed
- [ ] Evaluation areas assigned
- [ ] Feedback expectations established
- [ ] Candidate communication plan confirmed
- [ ] Risks documented

## Sample Intake Summary

**Role:** Operations Coordinator

**Hiring Manager:** Director of Operations

**Reason for Hire:** Team growth

**Target Start Date:** November

**Work Arrangement:** Hybrid

**Priority Qualifications:** Operations coordination, scheduling, documentation, stakeholder communication

**Interview Process:** Recruiter screen, hiring manager interview, team interview, final conversation

**Primary Risk:** Hiring manager availability during the interview period

**Next Action:** Finalize interviewer availability and open scheduling blocks.

## Process Improvement Questions

After several hiring cycles, review:

* Which intake information is frequently missing?
* Which approvals create delays?
* Are job requirements changing after interviews begin?
* Are interview stages necessary?
* Is candidate communication consistent?
* Are hiring managers providing timely feedback?
* Can any part of the process be standardized?

## Key Takeaway

A strong recruiting intake creates clarity before candidates enter the process.

When the role, decision makers, requirements, interview structure, timeline, and communication expectations are defined early, recruiting teams can move faster and create a more consistent candidate experience.
