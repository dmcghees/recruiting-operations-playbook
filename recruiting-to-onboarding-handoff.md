# Recruiting to Onboarding Handoff

## Purpose

The recruiting to onboarding handoff marks the transition from candidate to employee.

This guide outlines a practical process for transferring the information needed to begin onboarding while maintaining accuracy, privacy, and clear ownership.

The goal is to avoid delays between offer acceptance and Day One readiness.

## Why the Handoff Matters

Once a candidate accepts an offer, several teams may need to begin work.

These may include:

* Human Resources
* Workplace Operations
* Information Technology
* Security
* Payroll
* Hiring Manager
* People Operations

If the handoff is incomplete or delayed, the new employee may arrive without equipment, access, workspace, or accurate first day information.

## 1. Confirm Offer Acceptance

Before beginning the onboarding handoff, confirm:

* Offer has been formally accepted
* Start date is confirmed
* Role information is accurate
* Required internal approvals are complete

Do not begin downstream processes based only on an expected acceptance.

## 2. Confirm Employee Information

Transfer only the information required for onboarding.

This may include:

* Employee name
* Job title
* Department
* Manager
* Start date
* Work location
* Remote, hybrid, or onsite status
* Employment type

Sensitive recruiting information should not be distributed unnecessarily.

## 3. Confirm Hiring Manager Responsibilities

The hiring manager may need to provide:

* Workspace requirements
* Equipment needs
* Application access
* Team schedule
* First day meeting plan
* Role specific onboarding information

Identify missing information early.

## 4. Initiate Technology Requests

Provide the required information to Information Technology according to company procedures.

Potential requests may include:

* Laptop
* Monitor
* Docking equipment
* Email account
* Required software
* Shared drives
* Communication tools
* Other system access

Technology permissions should be approved by the appropriate owner.

## 5. Initiate Workplace Requests

For onsite or hybrid employees, Workplace Operations may coordinate:

* Desk assignment
* Workspace preparation
* Office supplies
* Building badge
* Keys when required
* Parking information
* Reception notification
* First day arrival instructions

## 6. Compliance and HR Tasks

Human Resources or other authorized teams may own items such as:

* Employment eligibility verification
* Payroll documentation
* Tax forms
* Policies
* Benefits enrollment
* Required acknowledgements

Recruiting Operations should track the handoff without storing sensitive documents in inappropriate systems.

## 7. Update Candidate Status

Once the offer is accepted:

* Update the recruiting system
* Record acceptance date
* Confirm start date
* Close unnecessary recruiting tasks
* Transfer required information to onboarding
* Preserve recruiting records according to company policy

## Handoff Checklist

- [ ] Offer accepted
- [ ] Start date confirmed
- [ ] Employee information verified
- [ ] Hiring manager notified
- [ ] Human Resources notified
- [ ] Technology request initiated
- [ ] Workplace request initiated
- [ ] Building access process initiated
- [ ] First day logistics assigned
- [ ] Recruiting system updated
- [ ] Outstanding risks documented

## Common Handoff Risks

Potential problems include:

* Start date changes not communicated
* Equipment requests submitted late
* Hiring manager information incomplete
* Work location entered incorrectly
* Badge request delayed
* Required application access missing
* Candidate information entered differently across systems

## Example

**Situation:** A candidate accepted an offer with a start date two weeks away, but equipment and access requests had not yet been initiated.

**Action:** Confirmed the accepted start date, transferred the required onboarding information, assigned owners to technology and workplace requests, and tracked readiness through the onboarding process.

**Result:** Outstanding items became visible early enough for the teams to prepare before the employee's first day.

## Key Takeaway

The recruiting process does not end when the candidate says yes.

A strong handoff ensures the information gathered during hiring becomes accurate, actionable onboarding work with clear ownership and enough time to prepare for Day One.
