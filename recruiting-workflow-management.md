# Recruiting Workflow Management

## Purpose

Recruiting workflow management provides visibility into where candidates are in the hiring process, what actions are required, and who owns the next step.

The goal is to prevent candidates, feedback, approvals, and hiring decisions from becoming stalled between stages.

## Typical Recruiting Workflow

A recruiting process may include:

1. Requisition opened
2. Candidate sourced or applied
3. Resume review
4. Initial screen
5. Hiring manager review
6. Interview process
7. Final evaluation
8. Offer approval
9. Offer extended
10. Offer accepted
11. Preboarding and onboarding handoff

The exact process may vary by role.

## 1. Define Hiring Stages

Each stage should have a clear meaning.

Example:

| Stage | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| New Application | Candidate has applied but has not been reviewed |
| Recruiter Review | Application under initial review |
| Recruiter Screen | Initial conversation scheduled or completed |
| Hiring Manager Review | Candidate submitted to manager |
| Interview | Candidate actively interviewing |
| Final Review | Interviews complete and decision pending |
| Offer | Offer process underway |
| Hired | Candidate accepted |
| Closed | Candidate no longer active |

## 2. Assign Ownership

Every active candidate should have a next action and an owner.

Example:

| Candidate | Stage | Next Action | Owner | Due Date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Candidate A | Recruiter Screen | Schedule interview | Recruiting Coordinator | Oct 5 |
| Candidate B | Manager Review | Review resume | Hiring Manager | Oct 5 |
| Candidate C | Interview | Submit feedback | Interview Panel | Oct 6 |

## 3. Track Candidate Movement

Candidates should not remain indefinitely in the same stage.

Regularly review:

* Candidates awaiting scheduling
* Candidates awaiting feedback
* Candidates awaiting hiring manager review
* Candidates awaiting final decisions
* Offers awaiting approval
* Candidates requiring updates

## 4. Identify Bottlenecks

Common recruiting bottlenecks include:

* Hiring manager review delays
* Interviewer availability
* Missing feedback
* Offer approval delays
* Compensation discussions
* Changing job requirements
* Too many interview stages

Tracking where candidates stall helps identify process problems.

## 5. Maintain Data Accuracy

Candidate records should reflect the actual status of the process.

Update:

* Interview dates
* Candidate stage
* Feedback status
* Withdrawals
* Rejections
* Offer status
* Final disposition

Accurate data improves reporting and reduces confusion.

## 6. Track Aging

Monitor how long candidates remain in each stage.

Example:

| Candidate | Stage | Days in Stage | Action |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Candidate A | Hiring Manager Review | 1 | No action |
| Candidate B | Hiring Manager Review | 5 | Follow up |
| Candidate C | Feedback Pending | 3 | Escalate |

Stage aging can reveal where the hiring process is slowing down.

## 7. Weekly Pipeline Review

A recruiting operations review may include:

* Open requisitions
* New candidates
* Interviews scheduled
* Candidates awaiting feedback
* Candidates awaiting decisions
* Offers in progress
* Upcoming start dates
* Process risks

## 8. Escalation

Escalate when:

* Candidate feedback is significantly overdue
* Interview scheduling cannot move forward
* An approval is blocking the process
* A candidate has been waiting without an update
* The hiring team changes the process midstream
* A high priority role is falling behind schedule

## Example Workflow Issue

**Problem:** Candidates were frequently waiting several days after interviews because interviewer feedback had not been submitted.

**Action:** Added feedback deadlines to interview communications and created a daily review of outstanding scorecards.

**Result:** Missing feedback became visible earlier and recruiting could follow up before candidates experienced longer delays.

## Useful Metrics

Workflow metrics may include:

* Time in stage
* Time to schedule
* Feedback completion time
* Candidate conversion between stages
* Interview reschedule rate
* Offer acceptance rate
* Candidate withdrawal rate

## Key Takeaway

A recruiting workflow should always make three things clear:

**Where is the candidate?**

**What happens next?**

**Who owns the next action?**

When those questions can be answered quickly, the hiring process becomes easier to manage and less likely to stall.
